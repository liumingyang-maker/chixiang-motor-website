CG Pushrod (OHV) vs CB Chain-Driven (OHC) Motorcycle Engines (125cc–250cc): Engineering & Market Selection Guide

Published: November 2026 · Engineering & Procurement Guide by CHIXIANG MOTOR

Quick Answer Verdict

Choose CHIXIANG CG pushrod (OHV) engines (124–229.5 cc) for utility commuters and rugged work fleets prioritizing simple top-end servicing and low-speed pulling torque. Choose CHIXIANG CB chain-driven (OHC) engines (149–223 cc) for commuter, trail, and off-road platforms requiring lighter mass (29–31 kg), compact height (452 mm), smoother valvetrain acoustics, and stronger 250cc-class output (12 kW, 17.5 N·m).

1. Verified Specification Comparison: 125cc, 150cc, 200cc & 250cc Tiers

When sourcing air-cooled four-stroke powertrains for assembly lines or regional distribution, buyers frequently evaluate our CG Pushrod Air-Cooled Engine Line against our CB Chain-Driven OHC Engine Line. Both platforms utilize a 5-speed constant-mesh transmission, yet their valvetrain layouts produce distinct torque curves, physical footprints, and unit weights.

Head-to-Head Tier Analysis

  • 125cc Utility Baseline: The CG125 (124 cc, 7.5 kW @ 8,000 rpm, 8.6 N·m @ 7,000 rpm, 9.2:1) anchors entry-level commercial commuter fleets where repair simplicity and minimal downtime outweigh peak velocity.
  • 150cc Commuter Matchup: Both the CG150 and CB150 displace 149 cc. The CG150 edges peak power (8.5 kW @ 8,000 rpm vs. 8.2 kW @ 8,500 rpm), while the CB150 delivers higher torque (10.5 N·m @ 7,000 rpm vs. 10 N·m @ 7,500 rpm) at 2.05 kg lower net weight (29 kg vs. 31.05 kg), favoring agile street and light-trail chassis.
  • 200cc Mid-Range Matchup: At 197 cc, both CG200 and CB200-C produce 13.5 N·m of maximum torque. However, the CB200-C reaches peak power (10.2 kW vs. 10 kW) and peak torque (6,000 rpm vs. 6,500 rpm) 500 rpm earlier, providing crisp midrange throttle response in a shorter vertical envelope (452 mm vs. 500 mm).
  • 250cc Performance vs. Grunt Matchup: Although the CG250 has a larger displacement (229.5 cc, 67 × 65 mm) and reaches its 16.5 N·m torque peak at a tractor-like 5,000 rpm for heavy utility loads, the CB250 (223 cc) leverages overhead-cam breathing to deliver 14.3% more power (12 kW vs. 10.5 kW @ 7,000 rpm) and higher peak torque (17.5 N·m @ 5,500 rpm) while weighing 1 kg less (31 kg vs. 32 kg).

2. Valvetrain Mechanics, Servicing Simplicity & Balance-Shaft Options

Pushrod OHV (CG Series) vs. Chain-Driven OHC (CB Series)

  • CG Pushrod (OHV) Architecture: Locates the camshaft low in the crankcase, actuating overhead valves via rigid pushrods and rocker arms. Eliminating a timing chain, cam-chain guides, and tensioner prevents chain-stretch failures under irregular oil-change intervals. Top-end cylinder and piston teardowns can be completed quickly in basic roadside workshops without resetting cam-chain timing.
  • CB Chain-Driven (OHC) Architecture: Positions the camshaft directly inside the cylinder head driven by a timing chain. Lower reciprocating valvetrain mass reduces high-rpm valve float, enables quieter mechanical acoustics, and supports stronger volumetric efficiency—especially evident in the 12 kW CB250 output. Routine maintenance focuses on periodic inspection of timing-chain tension and oil cleanliness.

Optional Balance-Shaft Variants

High-displacement single-cylinder engines generate primary vibration at sustained cruising speeds. CHIXIANG supplies optional balance-shaft configurations across designated CG air-cooled variants to reduce handlebar and footpeg vibration for commercial commuters and longer-distance riders (balance-shaft availability on specific CB builds is verified by engine code). Matching replacement valvetrain, clutch, and top-end service components for both architectures are supplied through our Engine Spare Parts Directory.

3. Pre-Order Frame, Mounting & Commercial Verification Checklist

Despite visual similarities between CG and CB air-cooled crankcases, direct drop-in interchangeability across chassis frames is never assumed without engineering verification. Importers and vehicle assemblers should confirm five interface points before locking a production order:

  1. Frame Clearance & Mounting Lug Geometry: Note the package height difference (500 mm CG carton vs. 452 mm CB carton). Verify upper cylinder-head stay brackets, lower crankcase mounting bolt spacing, and rear swingarm pivot alignment against your target frame blueprint before production.
  2. Compression Ratio & Local Fuel Grade: CG air-cooled compression spans 8.5–9.3:1 on CG200, 8.7:1 on CG250, and 9.2:1 on CG125/CG150 to accommodate varying regional octane grades; verify target fuel calibration for CB150, CB200-C, and CB250 orders.
  3. Transmission Pattern & Output Drive: Both families use a 5-speed constant-mesh gearbox. Specify international (1-N-2-3-4-5) versus rotary shift drums, kick-start/electric-start configuration, and front sprocket spline and chain pitch on the Order BOM.
  4. Electrical Harness & Ignition Specs: Verify stator plug pinouts, AC/DC CDI compatibility, gear-position indicator wiring, and high-output magneto charging requirements against your target market harness.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. When should a distributor choose CG pushrod engines over CB OHC engines?

Select CG pushrod (OHV) engines for rural commuter markets, cargo-utility bikes, and regions where mechanics rely on rapid, tool-minimal top-end rebuilds without cam-chain timing procedures. Select CB chain-driven (OHC) engines for off-road, dual-sport, and urban commuter models prioritizing lower weight (29–31 kg), compact height, quieter operation, and stronger 250cc performance (12 kW).

2. Can a CB chain-driven engine be installed directly into a CG motorcycle frame?

Not without verifying frame geometry first. While both use 5-speed constant-mesh gearboxes and similar external footprints, upper head-stay brackets, crankcase mounting boss offsets, exhaust port angles, and wiring harness connectors must be cross-checked against your chassis drawings prior to ordering.

3. Between CG250 and CB250, which engine delivers better performance?

The CB250 (223 cc, 31 kg) delivers higher peak power (12 kW @ 7,000 rpm) and higher peak torque (17.5 N·m @ 5,500 rpm), making it stronger for trail and highway-capable motorcycles. The CG250 (229.5 cc, 32 kg) reaches its 16.5 N·m torque peak 500 rpm earlier (5,000 rpm), suiting slower heavy-load utility riding.

4. Are balance-shaft versions and matching spare parts available?

Yes. CHIXIANG manufactures optional balance-shaft variants on designated CG air-cooled models to minimize engine vibration, and supplies matching factory service parts—including cylinder kits, cylinder heads, crankshafts, clutches, and gasket sets—for mixed-container replenishment.

5. What are the MOQ, lead time, and warranty terms for mixed CG and CB orders?

Standard wholesale MOQ is 50 complete engines, and buyers can mix CG125–CG250, CB150–CB250, and spare parts in the same shipment. Regular lead time is approximately 10 working days under FOB Guangzhou terms (other major Chinese ports by arrangement). All engines carry a 60-day warranty after destination port arrival, with free replacement parts shipped in the next container following engine serial number plus photo or video verification.
